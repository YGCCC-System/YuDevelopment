A guide to Yu Development's visual language — disciplined, institutional, and built for the long term. Derived directly from the live site.

Tone · Confident, civic, operator-led Not · Glossy, loud, brochure real estate
01

Position

Yu Development reads like a serious operator, not a sales brochure. The look is dark, spare, and capital-markets credible — restraint that signals discipline to investors and cities alike. Boldness here comes from scale and contrast, never from bright color or decoration.

Hold onto this: attainable housing for underbuilt markets, presented with conviction and zero gloss.

02

Logo

The wordmark is "YU DEVELOPMENT" set in a clean geometric sans, all caps, with generous letter-spacing (~0.34em). It behaves like a quiet nameplate. Default is white on navy; reverse to navy on light, or white on olive for accent applications.

Do

Keep the wide tracking. Maintain clear space around it. Default to white-on-navy. Scale tracking down slightly at very small sizes so letters don't detach.

Don't

Tighten into a normal-tracked logotype, add an icon or tagline lockup, set it in a serif, apply gradients, or place it on busy photography without a scrim.

03

Color

A dark, monochromatic navy system carries almost everything. White and muted blue-gray handle text. A single muted olive is the only true accent — pulled from the site's secondary button — used with discipline, mostly on secondary actions and small markers.

04

Typography

The signature is a sans + serif pairing: a geometric sans (Manrope) does the structural, confident work — headlines, the wordmark, UI — while a literary serif (Newsreader) carries the narrative voice, exactly as the site uses a serif for its "who we are" paragraphs. Both are open-source.

06

Imagery

Real, aerial, and grounded — like the site footage on the homepage. Photography shows actual developments and the markets around them, graded naturally. Let images run full-bleed behind a dark scrim so white type stays legible.

Use

  • Aerial and drone footage of real sites
  • Completed developments, occupied and lived-in
  • Natural, true-to-life color grading
  • Full-bleed imagery under a dark navy scrim
  • Wide context shots of the surrounding market

Avoid

  • Glass-tower renderings and CGI skylines
  • Handshake / smiling-team stock photos
  • Heavy filters or cold corporate grading
  • Busy photos behind text without a scrim
  • Decorative stock unrelated to the actual work
